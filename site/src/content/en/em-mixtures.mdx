<Section lang="en" k="scenario">

Chapter 10 split the patients into subtypes with k-means, but the doctors were unhappy on two counts. k-means only recognizes round clusters, while real subtypes may be elongated or tilted. And it only says yes or no, while a patient near a boundary is plainly "70% like A, 30% like B".

Think of it differently: each subtype is a Gaussian distribution; a patient first belongs to a subtype (with some probability), then has their markers "generated" from that Gaussian. The subtype itself is unseen: a **latent variable**.

This chapter answers:

1. With a latent variable the likelihood contains a sum over it and cannot be solved directly. What then? (The EM algorithm.)
2. Why does every EM step increase the likelihood? (The ELBO view.)
3. How does EM relate to k-means, to Chapter 09's Baum–Welch, and to the variational autoencoders to come?

### Gaussian mixture models

<Box kind="definition" title="Gaussian mixture model (GMM)">

**Model**: a latent $z \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ with $P(z = k) = \pi_k$; given $z = k$, $x \sim \mathcal N(\mu_k, \Sigma_k)$. Hence

$$
p(x \mid \theta) = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k\,\mathcal N(x; \mu_k, \Sigma_k).
$$

**Strategy**: maximize the marginal log-likelihood $\ell(\theta) = \sum_i \log \sum_k \pi_k\,\mathcal N(x_i; \mu_k, \Sigma_k)$.

**Algorithm (EM)**: alternate two steps:

- **E-step**: with the current parameters compute the **responsibilities** $r_{ik} = P(z_i = k \mid x_i, \theta) = \dfrac{\pi_k\mathcal N(x_i; \mu_k, \Sigma_k)}{\sum_j \pi_j\mathcal N(x_i; \mu_j, \Sigma_j)}$;
- **M-step**: use the responsibilities as weights and estimate as if labels were known: $N_k = \sum_i r_{ik}$, $\pi_k = N_k/n$, $\mu_k = \frac1{N_k}\sum_i r_{ik}x_i$, $\Sigma_k = \frac1{N_k}\sum_i r_{ik}(x_i - \mu_k)(x_i - \mu_k)^\top$.

</Box>

The difficulty is the sum inside the log: setting the derivative with respect to $\mu_k$ to zero gives an equation in which $r_{ik}$ itself depends on $\mu_k$, so there is no closed form. EM's idea: **if we knew which cluster each point belongs to, this would be Chapter 05's Gaussian discriminant analysis, solved in one pass**. Since we don't, guess a "soft" answer (E-step), solve under that answer (M-step), and use the new parameters to improve the guess.

A few practical points:

- The likelihood can be unbounded: if one Gaussian collapses onto a single point with variance going to 0, $\ell \to \infty$. Implementations add a small regularizer to the covariances.
- EM only reaches a local maximum (or saddle point) and depends on the start; it is commonly started from a k-means solution, with several restarts.
- The number of components $K$ is chosen by BIC or cross-validated likelihood.

### The general EM algorithm

For any latent-variable model $p(x, z \mid \theta)$ the goal is to maximize $\log p(x \mid \theta) = \log\sum_z p(x, z \mid \theta)$:

- **E-step**: compute the posterior $q(z) = p(z \mid x, \theta^{(t)})$ and form $Q(\theta) = \mathbb E_{q}[\log p(x, z \mid \theta)]$;
- **M-step**: $\theta^{(t+1)} = \arg\max_\theta Q(\theta)$.

The complete-data log-likelihood is usually simple (for exponential families, "sufficient statistics times parameters"), so the M-step often has a closed form, as for GMMs and Chapter 09's Baum–Welch.

### The ELBO: a lower bound on the log-likelihood

For any distribution $q(z)$ over $z$,

$$
\log p(x \mid \theta) = \underbrace{\sum_z q(z)\log\frac{p(x, z \mid \theta)}{q(z)}}_{\mathrm{ELBO}(q,\ \theta)} + \underbrace{\sum_z q(z)\log\frac{q(z)}{p(z \mid x, \theta)}}_{\mathrm{KL}(q\ \|\ p(z \mid x, \theta))}.
$$

**Derivation**: write $\log p(x \mid \theta)$ as $\sum_z q(z)\log p(x \mid \theta)$ (since $q$ sums to 1), use $p(x \mid \theta) = p(x, z \mid \theta)/p(z \mid x, \theta)$, then multiply and divide by $q(z)$ and split into two terms. The KL divergence is non-negative (Jensen), so the ELBO is a **lower bound** on $\log p(x \mid \theta)$ (the evidence lower bound), with equality exactly when $q$ is the posterior.

<Box kind="theorem" title="EM never decreases the likelihood">

EM is **coordinate ascent** on the ELBO:

- the **E-step** fixes $\theta^{(t)}$ and maximizes over $q$: taking $q = p(z \mid x, \theta^{(t)})$ makes the KL 0, so the bound **touches** $\log p$ at $\theta^{(t)}$;
- the **M-step** fixes $q$ and maximizes the ELBO over $\theta$ (the entropy of $q$ does not depend on $\theta$, so this is maximizing $Q(\theta)$), **pushing** the bound up.

Therefore

$$
\log p(x \mid \theta^{(t+1)}) \ \ge\ \mathrm{ELBO}(q, \theta^{(t+1)}) \ \ge\ \mathrm{ELBO}(q, \theta^{(t)}) = \log p(x \mid \theta^{(t)}).
$$

</Box>

<ElboBound client:visible lang="en" />

The chain still holds as long as the M-step increases the ELBO (no need to maximize it). That is **generalized EM (GEM)**: the M-step may be a single gradient step or one round of coordinate ascent. Going further, write the ELBO as $F(q, \theta)$: EM is the **maximization–maximization algorithm** for $F$, maximizing one function alternately over $q$ and $\theta$.

**The road to VAEs**: when the posterior $p(z \mid x, \theta)$ cannot be computed (say, the decoder is a neural network), the E-step cannot be done exactly. Variational inference instead searches a tractable family for the $q$ closest to the posterior, maximizing the ELBO with a KL that is no longer 0. The variational autoencoder (Chapter 24) goes one step further: a neural network $q_\phi(z \mid x)$ produces $q$ for every data point at once (**amortized**), and is trained together with $\theta$ by gradient ascent on the ELBO.

### The GMM E-step and M-step

The E-step is Bayes' rule (derivation exercise 1). The M-step maximizes

$$
Q(\theta) = \sum_i\sum_k r_{ik}\big[\log\pi_k + \log\mathcal N(x_i; \mu_k, \Sigma_k)\big].
$$

- In $\mu_k$: $\sum_i r_{ik}\Sigma_k^{-1}(x_i - \mu_k) = 0$ ⇒ $\mu_k = \sum_i r_{ik}x_i / N_k$, a weighted mean (derivation exercise 2).
- In $\pi_k$ with the constraint $\sum_k \pi_k = 1$, a Lagrange multiplier gives $\pi_k = N_k/n$ (derivation exercise 3).
- In $\Sigma_k$: the weighted sample covariance.

### k-means is the hard-assignment limit

Take all $\Sigma_k = \sigma^2 I$ and equal weights. With two components, the responsibility of the second for $x$ is

$$
r_2(x) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\!\Big(-\dfrac{(\mu_2 - \mu_1)^\top\big(x - \tfrac{\mu_1 + \mu_2}{2}\big)}{\sigma^2}\Big)}
$$

(derivation exercise 4, one-dimensional case): a sigmoid centred on the midpoint of the two centres. As $\sigma^2 \to 0$ it becomes a step, every point belongs entirely to its nearest centre, and the M-step's weighted mean becomes the cluster mean. That is k-means.

### Worked example: one EM step

<Box kind="example" title="Four points, two unit-variance Gaussians">

Data $-1, 0, 3, 4$; the model has two components with variance fixed at 1, initial weights $\tfrac12$ and initial means $\mu_1 = 0$, $\mu_2 = 2$.

**E-step**. With equal weights and variances, $r_{i2} = \sigma(2x_i - 2)$:

| $x_i$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $3$ | $4$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $r_{i2}$ | $0.018$ | $0.119$ | $0.982$ | $0.998$ |

**M-step**. $N_2 = 2.117$ and $N_1 = 1.883$:

- $\mu_1 = \dfrac{\sum_i r_{i1}x_i}{N_1} \approx -0.488$ and $\mu_2 = \dfrac{\sum_i r_{i2}x_i}{N_2} \approx 3.268$;
- $\pi = (0.471,\ 0.529)$.

The log-likelihood rises from $-9.283$ to $-7.001$; one more iteration gives $-6.943$, with the means heading for $-0.5$ and $3.5$, the means of the two groups of data.

</Box>

### Extensions

- **Choosing $K$ and the covariance structure by BIC**: $\mathrm{BIC} = -2\ell + d\log n$ with $d$ free parameters; scikit-learn's `GaussianMixture` offers `full`, `tied`, `diag` and `spherical` covariances, `tied` being LDA's shared covariance.
- **Missing data**: EM was designed for incomplete data in the first place (Dempster, Laird and Rubin, 1977): treat the missing features as latent variables.
- **Variational Bayesian GMMs** put priors on the parameters too and use variational inference, which can switch off superfluous components (`BayesianGaussianMixture`).
- **From EM to deep generative models**: the VAE (Chapter 24) uses a neural network as an amortized E-step; the training objective of diffusion models (Chapter 25) is an ELBO as well.
