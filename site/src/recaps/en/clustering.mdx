- **k-means**: minimize the within-cluster sum of squares $J = \sum_i \|x_i - \mu_{c(i)}\|^2$; Lloyd alternates "assign to the nearest centre / move centres to cluster means": coordinate descent.
- **Monotone and convergent**: $\sum_{i\in C}\|x_i - \mu\|^2 = \sum\|x_i - \bar x\|^2 + n\|\bar x - \mu\|^2$ ⇒ the update is optimal; the assignment is optimal point by point; finitely many partitions ⇒ finite convergence, but only to a local optimum.
- **k-means++**: seed by $D(x)^2$ sampling, with $\mathbb E\,J \le 8(\ln K + 2)J^*$; add several restarts.
- **Hierarchical clustering**: agglomerative merging, cut the dendrogram at any height; single (closest pair, chains), complete (farthest pair, compact), average, Ward (consistent with the k-means objective).
- **Worked examples**: $1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 12$ from centres $1, 2$: $J$ goes $165 \to 59.2 \to 39.04 \to 33.25 \to 25.44 \to 56/3$, ending at $\{1,2,4\}, \{7,8,12\}$; for $1, 2, 4, 7, 11$, single linkage merges at $1,2,3,4$, complete at $1,3,6,10$.
- **Link with GMMs**: equal spherical Gaussians with $\sigma \to 0$ turn soft responsibilities into hard assignments ⇒ k-means is the limit of GMM-EM (Chapter 11).
- **Choosing k / evaluating**: elbow, silhouette $s = (b-a)/\max(a,b)$, gap statistic; with labels, ARI and NMI. **Standardize first.**
