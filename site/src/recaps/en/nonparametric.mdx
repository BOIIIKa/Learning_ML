- **kNN**: majority vote (or average) of the $k$ nearest points; the three choices are the distance, $k$ and the decision rule. Small $k$ = complex, high variance (zero training error at $k = 1$); large $k$ = smooth, high bias; about $n/k$ effective parameters. Standardize first.
- **Distances**: $L_p$; the $L_1$ ball is a diamond, $L_2$ a circle, $L_\infty$ a square. Worked example: from the origin, $(1.5, 1.5)$ is at $L_\infty$ distance 1.5, closer than $(2, 0)$ at 2, so the nearest neighbour depends on the distance.
- **kd-tree**: split at the median, cycling through coordinates; search descends to a leaf, then on the way back visits the far side only if the ball crosses the splitting plane ($|q_a - s_a| \lt$ best distance). Worked example: 4 of 6 points visited, the whole right subtree pruned.
- **Cover–Hart**: as $n \to \infty$, $R^* \le R_{1\text{NN}} \le 2R^*(1-R^*) \le 2R^*$; the pointwise error is $2\eta(1-\eta)$.
- **Curse of dimensionality**: shell fraction $1 - (1-\varepsilon)^d \to 1$; a cube holding a fraction $r$ needs edge $r^{1/d} \to 1$; nearest/farthest distance ratio $\to 1$. Local methods need exponentially many samples, and kd-tree pruning fails.
- **Splines**: the cubic regression-spline basis $1, x, x^2, x^3, (x-\xi_k)^3_+$ has $K + 4$ degrees of freedom; steadier than a polynomial with the same df (no Runge oscillation). Natural splines are linear at the ends; smoothing splines solve $\min \sum(y - f)^2 + \lambda\int f''^2$ with $\mathrm{df} = \mathrm{tr}\,S_\lambda$.
- **GAM**: $f(x) = \beta_0 + \sum_j f_j(x_j)$, fitted by backfitting; interpretable non-linearity. In scikit-learn, `SplineTransformer` + a linear model does it.
