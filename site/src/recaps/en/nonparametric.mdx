- **kNN's triad**: the model is local averaging (the training set is the model), the strategy is a majority vote / mean within the neighbourhood, the algorithm is neighbour search. Three choices: the distance (L1 / L2 / L∞, standardize first), k (small k: low bias, high variance; pick it by cross-validation) and the decision rule.
- **Cover–Hart**: $R^* \le R_{1\text{NN}} \le 2R^*(1-R^*) \le 2R^*$; with $k\to\infty$ and $k/n\to0$ kNN is consistent.
- **kd-tree**: median cuts on alternating coordinates, built in $O(n\log n)$. The search walks down to a leaf, then on the way back prunes any subtree whose splitting line is at least $r$ from $q$: $O(\log n)$ on average in low dimensions.
- **Curse of dimensionality**: the shell's share of volume $1-(1-\varepsilon)^d\to1$; holding a fraction $p$ of the data takes an edge of $p^{1/d}$; the relative spread of distances $\sqrt{7/(5d)}\to0$, so the nearest/farthest ratio tends to 1.
- **Basis expansions**: $f(x)=\sum_m\beta_m h_m(x)$ is linear in $\beta$, so least squares applies unchanged. A degree-$M$ spline with $K$ knots has $M+1+K$ degrees of freedom (cubic: $K+4$; natural cubic: $K$).
- **Smoothing splines and GAMs**: $\min\sum(y_i-f(x_i))^2+\lambda\int f''^2$ is solved by a natural cubic spline, $\hat\beta=(N^\top N+\lambda\Omega)^{-1}N^\top y$; the effective df is $\operatorname{tr}S$ ($n/k$ for kNN). GAM: $f=\alpha+\sum_j f_j(x_j)$, fitted by backfitting or a single ridge regression.
