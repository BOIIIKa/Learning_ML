- **Generative vs discriminative**: a generative model fits $p(x, y) = \pi_k\,p(x \mid k)$ and gets $P(y \mid x)$ by Bayes' rule; a discriminative one (logistic regression) models $P(y \mid x)$ directly.
- **The prior only shifts the intercept**: log-odds $= \log(\pi_1/\pi_0) + \log\frac{p(x\mid 1)}{p(x\mid 0)}$; a new prior needs no retraining.
- **QDA**: $\mathcal N(\mu_k, \Sigma_k)$ per class; quadratic term $-\frac12 x^\top(\Sigma_1^{-1} - \Sigma_0^{-1})x$ ⇒ conic boundary; $p(p+1)/2$ covariance parameters per class.
- **LDA**: shared $\Sigma$ ⇒ linear boundary with $w = \Sigma^{-1}(\mu_1 - \mu_0)$; the posterior is exactly $\sigma(w^\top x + b)$, the same family as logistic regression with a different estimator (joint vs conditional likelihood). In 1-D the threshold is $x^* = \frac{\mu_0+\mu_1}{2} + \frac{\sigma^2}{\mu_1-\mu_0}\log\frac{\pi_0}{\pi_1}$.
- **Naive Bayes**: features independent given the class, $p(x\mid k) = \prod_j p(x_j \mid k)$; $O(p)$ parameters, training is counting; often accurate, often overconfident.
- **Laplace smoothing**: $\frac{n_k(x_j = v) + \alpha}{n_k + S_j\alpha}$, the MAP estimate under a Beta/Dirichlet prior; it removes zero probabilities. Worked example with $\alpha = 1$: $P(+\mid \neg F, \neg C) = 32/437 \approx 0.073$ (0 unsmoothed), and $P(+\mid F, C)$ goes from 0.9 to $320/401 \approx 0.798$.
- **Sample efficiency**: when its assumptions hold, the generative model converges faster (naive Bayes $O(\log p)$ vs logistic $O(p)$); when they fail, the discriminative model has the lower limit, and the learning curves cross (Ng and Jordan 2001).
