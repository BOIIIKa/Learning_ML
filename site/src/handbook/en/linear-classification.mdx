### Traps

- **It is regularized by default.** `LogisticRegression` uses `C=1.0`, an L2 penalty $\frac{1}{2C}\|w\|^2$, not the textbook maximum likelihood. For statistical inference (standard errors, p-values) use statsmodels' `Logit`; for an essentially unregularized fit, make `C` very large.
- **Scale first.** Both the penalty and lbfgs's convergence depend on the scale. On a `ConvergenceWarning`, check for a `StandardScaler` before raising `max_iter`.
- **Perfect separation blows up the coefficients.** When a feature (or combination) separates the classes perfectly, the unregularized MLE does not exist: huge coefficients, infinite standard errors. It is often a sign of data leakage, so look at the data first.
- **Multiclass**: lbfgs and the other default solvers fit a softmax (multinomial) model, one weight vector per class; `coef_` has shape (classes, features).
- **The threshold is a decision, not part of the model.** In screening, missing a sick patient costs far more than an extra follow-up, so pick the threshold from the costs (recall Bayes decisions in Chapter 01); with imbalanced classes, `class_weight="balanced"` is another option. Choose the threshold on validation data, never on the test set.

### Reading coefficients and probabilities

- $e^{w_j}$ is an **odds ratio**: with the other features fixed, one more unit of $x_j$ multiplies the odds by $e^{w_j}$. After standardization, one unit is one standard deviation.
- Regularization shrinks probabilities towards 0.5. When the probabilities matter (say, risks shown to a doctor), check calibration with `CalibrationDisplay` and fix it with `CalibratedClassifierCV` if needed.
- In `SGDClassifier` only `loss="log_loss"` and `"modified_huber"` offer `predict_proba`; the hinge loss (SVM) gives scores, not probabilities.

### Frontier reading

- Soudry, Hoffer, Nacson, Gunasekar, Srebro (2018): the implicit bias of gradient descent on separable data; the direction converges to the max-margin one.
- Ng and Jordan (2001): discriminative versus generative classifiers, logistic regression versus naive Bayes.
- Berger, Della Pietra and Della Pietra (1996): the maximum-entropy approach to natural language processing, the classic reference for maxent models.
- Freund and Schapire (1999): large-margin classification with the perceptron (voted and averaged perceptrons).
- Guo et al. (2017): calibration of modern neural networks; temperature scaling is a one-parameter logistic regression on the logits.
