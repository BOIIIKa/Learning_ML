### Traps that bite

- **Standardise features first**: gradient descent is extremely sensitive to scale (in this chapter's scenario the condition number goes from 169 to 1). Trees do not care; linear models and networks do.
- **Finding a learning rate**: start tiny, multiply the rate by a constant every step, plot the loss, and take about 1/10 of the rate where the loss falls fastest (the LR range test). When training diverges, divide the rate by 3 before switching optimizers.
- **Adam's `weight_decay` is L2; AdamW's is decoupled decay**: PyTorch's `Adam(weight_decay=…)` adds $\lambda\theta$ to the gradient, where $\sqrt v$ dilutes it; use `AdamW`. Biases and LayerNorm parameters are usually not decayed.
- **Switching from SGD to momentum**: momentum 0.9 makes the effective step 10× longer; shrink the learning rate to match.
- **Larger batches**: scale the learning rate with the batch and add warm-up; with tiny batches, raising Adam's $\epsilon$ (default 1e-8) to 1e-6 sometimes stabilises training.
- **Schedule per step, not per epoch**: `OneCycleLR` and cosine schedules usually expect `sched.step()` once per minibatch; calling it once per epoch makes the warm-up absurdly long.
- **Gradient clipping**: the occasional huge gradient in RNN / Transformer training can throw the parameters far away; `clip_grad_norm_` is cheap insurance.
- **NaN losses**: check the data first (division by zero, log 0), then the learning rate, and only then numerical precision (use a GradScaler with mixed precision).

### Frontier reading

- Kingma & Ba (2015): Adam.
- Loshchilov & Hutter (2019): AdamW, decoupled weight decay; (2017) SGDR, cosine annealing with warm restarts.
- Goyal et al. (2017): the linear scaling rule and warm-up for large-batch training.
- Cohen et al. (2021): gradient descent on networks operates at the edge of stability.
- Foret et al. (2021): Sharpness-Aware Minimization.
- Chen et al. (2023): Lion, a sign-momentum optimizer; Defazio et al. (2024): schedule-free optimization.
