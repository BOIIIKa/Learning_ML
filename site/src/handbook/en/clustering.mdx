### Traps

- **Scale first**: the feature with the largest units decides everything. On the wine data, k-means' ARI against the true cultivars changes by more than 0.5 with and without standardization.
- **Several restarts**: use `n_init` of at least 10; a single run can stop at a local optimum with two centres crowded together.
- **k-means' shape assumption**: it suits clusters of similar size and roughly spherical shape. Whiten elongated clusters or use a GMM; for non-convex, noisy data use DBSCAN or spectral clustering.
- **Large data**: `MiniBatchKMeans` is an order of magnitude faster; hierarchical clustering needs $O(n^2)$ memory, so be careful beyond tens of thousands of points.
- **Clustering always answers**: uniformly random data can also be split into k clusters. Check that the clusters really exist with silhouettes, stability (re-cluster bootstrap samples and see whether the result holds) and domain knowledge.
