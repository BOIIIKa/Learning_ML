### Go-to code

```python
from sklearn.discriminant_analysis import LinearDiscriminantAnalysis, QuadraticDiscriminantAnalysis
from sklearn.naive_bayes import GaussianNB, MultinomialNB, BernoulliNB
from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import CountVectorizer
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline
from sklearn.model_selection import cross_val_score

lda = LinearDiscriminantAnalysis(solver="lsqr", shrinkage="auto")   # shrunk covariance, stable even when p ≈ n
qda = QuadraticDiscriminantAnalysis(reg_param=0.1)                   # shrink towards the identity
text = make_pipeline(CountVectorizer(min_df=2), MultinomialNB(alpha=1.0))
print(cross_val_score(lda, X, y, cv=5).mean())
```

### When to try them first

- **Few samples, moderate number of features**: LDA is often as good as logistic regression, in closed form and with no hyperparameters. `shrinkage="auto"` (Ledoit–Wolf) is almost always worth turning on.
- **Text, counts, huge numbers of features**: multinomial naive Bayes is a strong baseline that trains in seconds; use it to set the bar before anything fancier.
- **Classes with clearly different shapes** (one spread out, one tight): try QDA, with `reg_param` to keep the covariances invertible.

### Traps

- **`alpha` must not be 0**: in MultinomialNB/BernoulliNB, `alpha` is the Laplace smoothing; 0 brings back the zero-probability problem.
- **GaussianNB's `var_smoothing`** adds a small fraction of the largest variance to every variance, so no feature has zero variance within a class.
- **Priors**: `priors=` (LDA, QDA, GaussianNB) or `class_prior=` (MultinomialNB) swaps the prior directly; deploying to a hospital with a different follow-up rate, this beats retraining.
- **Probabilities are not trustworthy**: naive Bayes' `predict_proba` is often close to 0 or 1. Calibrate with `CalibratedClassifierCV` when you need probabilities.
- **LDA reduces dimension too**: `LinearDiscriminantAnalysis(n_components=K-1).fit_transform(X, y)` gives a supervised low-dimensional projection, handy for plots.

### Frontier reading

- Ng and Jordan (2001): discriminative versus generative classifiers, the classic paper on crossing learning curves.
- Friedman (1989): regularized discriminant analysis, interpolating between LDA and QDA.
- Ledoit and Wolf (2004): shrinkage estimation of large covariance matrices, the source of `shrinkage="auto"`.
- Rennie et al. (2003): fixing systematic biases of naive Bayes for text (complement naive Bayes, `ComplementNB` in scikit-learn).
