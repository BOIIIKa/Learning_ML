### kNN traps

- **Unscaled distances are meaningless**: the feature with the largest units dominates. Always add a `StandardScaler` (or weight features using domain knowledge).
- **Categorical features**: Euclidean distance on one-hot columns is awkward; consider Gower's distance or a different model.
- **`algorithm`**: scikit-learn picks between `kd_tree`, `ball_tree` and `brute`; above roughly 20 dimensions the trees barely help. For large-scale retrieval use approximate-neighbour libraries such as FAISS or HNSW.
- **Slow predictions, big memory**: kNN stores all the training data and does its work at prediction time. Keep that in mind for online serving.
- **Pick k by cross-validation**, starting around $\sqrt n$; with imbalanced classes use `weights="distance"` or adjust the decision threshold.

### Splines and GAMs

- `SplineTransformer` expands each feature into B-splines separately; a linear model on top is an additive model. `n_knots` sets the flexibility of each curve, the ridge penalty the overall smoothness.
- For interactions between features, add `PolynomialFeatures(interaction_only=True)` or switch to trees (Chapter 08).
- Beware of extrapolation: splines continue as polynomials outside the training range (`extrapolation="linear"` is safer).
- Dedicated GAM libraries (pyGAM, R's mgcv) choose the smoothness automatically with penalties and REML, and give confidence bands.

### Frontier reading

- Cover and Hart (1967): the asymptotic error bound of the nearest-neighbour rule.
- Beyer et al. (1999): "When is nearest neighbor meaningful?", on distances losing contrast in high dimension.
- Malkov and Yashunin (2018): HNSW, the most common approximate-neighbour index in vector databases.
- Hastie and Tibshirani (1986): generalized additive models; Wood's (2017) textbook covers mgcv in depth.
- Agarwal et al. (2021): neural additive models (NAMs), bringing GAM interpretability to deep learning.
