<Slide lang="en" part="method" layout="center" title="Hidden Markov models" say="Three sets of parameters and two assumptions.">

<Step say="An initial distribution pi, a transition matrix A, an emission matrix B.">$\lambda = (\pi, A, B)$</Step>

<Step say="Two assumptions: tomorrow depends only on today; today's test depends only on today's condition.">Markov property + observation independence</Step>

<Step say="So the joint probability of a path and its observations is a chain of products.">$P(o, s) = \pi_{s_1}B_{s_1}(o_1)\prod_t A_{s_{t-1}s_t}B_{s_t}(o_t)$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="The forward recursion" say="How do we get P of O without enumerating paths?">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="The forward variable: the first t observations, and state j on day t.">

$$
\alpha_{t+1}(j) = P(o_1, \dots, o_{t+1},\ s_{t+1} = j)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Condition on yesterday's state with the law of total probability.">

$$
\alpha_{t+1}(j) = \sum_i P(o_1, \dots, o_t,\ s_t = i)\,A_{ij}\,B_j(o_{t+1})
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="The bracket is the previous column of forward variables.">

$$
\alpha_{t+1}(j) = \Big[\sum_i \alpha_t(i)A_{ij}\Big]B_j(o_{t+1})
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="Replace the sum by a max, and you have Viterbi.">Viterbi: $\delta_{t+1}(j) = \max_i[\delta_t(i)A_{ij}]\,B_j(o_{t+1})$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="method" title="Baum–Welch: learning without labels" say="Test results only, no condition labels: how do we learn the parameters?">

<Step drive="s1=0/0.6; s1=10/4" say="The E-step runs forward–backward to get each day's state posteriors as soft counts; the M-step counts as usual.">E-step: soft counts $\gamma$, $\xi$; M-step: count and normalize</Step>

<Step drive="s1=40/4" say="The log-likelihood keeps rising and the parameters approach the truth.">Likelihood never decreases (EM, Chapter 11)</Step>

<Step say="The transition update: the expected number of moves from i to j, over the expected time spent in i.">$\hat A_{ij} = \sum_t \xi_t(i,j) / \sum_t \gamma_t(i)$</Step>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="From HMMs to CRFs" say="An HMM is generative; a CRF is discriminative.">

<Step drive="click:chip1" say="An HMM is a directed graph: it generates the state chain and lets each state emit an observation, modelling the joint distribution.">HMM: models $P(x, y)$, factors are conditional probabilities</Step>

<Step drive="click:chip2" say="A linear-chain CRF is undirected: it models only the conditional distribution, and its scores may look at the whole observation sequence.">CRF: models $P(y\mid x) = \exp(\sum w\cdot f)/Z(x)$</Step>

<Step say="The normalizer Z uses the same forward recursion, in log space, still at cost T N squared.">$Z(x)$: forward recursion (log-sum-exp), $O(TN^2)$</Step>
