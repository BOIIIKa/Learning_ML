<Slide lang="en" part="method" layout="center" title="Four explicit regularisers" say="Four explicit regularisers.">

<Step say="Weight decay: every step first shrinks the weights by a fixed factor, then follows the gradient.">Weight decay: $\theta \leftarrow (1 - \eta\lambda)\theta - \eta\nabla L$</Step>

<Step say="Dropout: drop random units during training and scale up the rest, so the expectation is unchanged.">Dropout: $\tilde h_j = m_j h_j / (1 - p)$</Step>

<Step say="Augmentation: transform the inputs without changing the label, for example by adding a little noise.">Augmentation: $x \to x + \varepsilon$</Step>

<Step say="Early stopping: watch the validation error, stop when it stops falling, and go back to the best step.">Early stopping: stop at the lowest validation error</Step>

<Step effect="focus" say="None of them removes a single parameter. They limit which functions training can reach.">
<p class="big-q sm">They limit the capacity in use, not the parameter count</p>
</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Early stopping and ridge" say="In linear regression, early stopping can be compared with ridge exactly.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="From zero, each gradient step multiplies the distance to the optimum by I minus eta A.">

$$
w_t - w^* = (I - \eta A)(w_{t-1} - w^*)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Unroll t steps: gradient descent has recovered part of the optimum.">

$$
w_t = \big[I - (I - \eta A)^t\big]\, w^*
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Along eigenvalue lambda, the fraction recovered is one minus one-minus-eta-lambda to the t.">

$$
\varphi^{\text{GD}}_t = 1 - (1 - \eta\lambda)^t \approx \eta t \lambda
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Ridge recovers lambda over lambda plus alpha, about lambda over alpha for small eigenvalues.">

$$
\varphi^{\text{ridge}}_\alpha = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda + \alpha} \approx \frac{\lambda}{\alpha}
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step effect="focus" say="They match when alpha is one over eta t: the number of steps plays the role of one over alpha.">
<p class="big-q sm">$\alpha = 1/(\eta t)$: steps $t$ act as $1/\alpha$</p>
</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="Early stopping ≈ ridge" say="Two eigenvalues, 1 and 0.04. Solid is gradient descent, dashed is ridge.">

<Step drive="s1=0.3/2" say="At first gradient descent has only covered the steep direction; the flat one has barely moved.">$t = 2$: only the steep direction</Step>

<Step drive="s1=1.7/3" say="Fifty steps, matching alpha 0.04: along the small eigenvalue one recovers 0.636, the other 0.5.">$t = 50$: $0.636$ vs $0.5$</Step>

<Step drive="s1=2.6/3" say="Run long enough and both reach the least-squares solution; the two paths nearly coincide all the way.">$t \to \infty$ ⇔ $\alpha \to 0$</Step>

<Step effect="focus" say="Both keep the directions with strong signal and suppress those that noise pushes around most.">
<p class="big-q sm">Early stopping = suppress small eigenvalues</p>
</Step>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Dropout and input noise" say="What does dropout do to a linear model?">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="The expected squared error is the squared bias plus the variance.">

$$
\mathbb E\big[(y - \hat y)^2\big] = (y - \mathbb E\hat y)^2 + \mathrm{Var}(\hat y)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="The mean is unchanged; each mask contributes variance p over one minus p.">

$$
\mathbb E\big[(y - \hat y)^2\big] = (y - w^\top x)^2 + \frac{p}{1 - p}\sum_j w_j^2 x_j^2
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="An L2 weighted by feature scale: features that are often large get their weights squeezed harder.">An adaptive L2</Step>

<Step say="Another view: two to the n subnetworks sharing weights, averaged at test time.">An ensemble of $2^n$ subnetworks</Step>

<Step say="Input noise does the same: for a linear model it adds exactly sigma squared times the squared norm.">Input noise: $+\,\sigma^2 \lVert w\rVert^2$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Why the minimum norm" say="Why is the limit from the origin always the minimum-norm solution?">

<Step say="Gradients always lie in the row space of X, so the limit does too.">$w_\infty = X^\top (X X^\top)^{-1} y \in \mathrm{row}(X)$</Step>

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="Any other solution is the limit plus a vector u from the null space.">

$$
w = w_\infty + u, \qquad Xu = 0
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="The null space is orthogonal to the row space: Pythagoras.">

$$
\lVert w\rVert^2 = \lVert w_\infty\rVert^2 + \lVert u\rVert^2 \ge \lVert w_\infty\rVert^2
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="Chapter 2's double descent was drawn with exactly this minimum-norm solution.">Chapter 02's double descent</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="SGD noise and flat minima" say="Another implicit regulariser comes from the step size and the noise.">

<Step say="In a valley of curvature h, each step multiplies the deviation by one minus eta h: it only settles if eta h is below two.">$\lvert 1 - \eta h\rvert \lt 1 \iff \eta h \lt 2$</Step>

<Step say="So gradient descent with step eta can only stay in minima whose largest curvature is below two over eta.">It can only stay where $\lambda_{\max}(H) \lt 2/\eta$</Step>

<Step say="Minibatches add noise that grows with eta over B, kicking it out of sharp minima more easily.">Noise $\propto \eta / B$ ⇒ a lean towards flat</Step>

<Step say="But beware: scale one layer by c and the next by one over c, and the function is unchanged while sharpness changes at will.">Reparametrisation: flatness must be scale-free</Step>

</Slide>
