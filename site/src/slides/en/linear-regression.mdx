<Slide lang="en" part="method" layout="center" title="Least squares and two penalties" say="Three objective functions.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="Least squares minimizes the residual sum of squares.">

$$
\min_\beta\ \|y - X\beta\|^2
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Set the gradient in β to zero.">

$$
-2X^\top(y - X\beta) = 0
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Rearranging gives the normal equations.">

$$
X^\top X\beta = X^\top y
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="With full column rank, solve for β.">

$$
\hat\beta = (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top y
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="Ridge adds lambda times the squared norm. The matrix becomes invertible.">Ridge: $(X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1}X^\top y$</Step>

<Step say="The lasso adds lambda times the absolute values. There is no closed form, but many coefficients become exactly zero.">Lasso: $+\lambda\|\beta\|_1$, exact zeros</Step>

<Step say="Always standardize features first and leave the intercept unpenalized.">Standardize first; don't penalize the intercept</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="method" title="Diamond versus disc" say="Why does the lasso give zeros? Look at the constraint form.">

<Step drive="s1=0/1.2; s1=1.6/3" say="The solution is where the loss contour first touches the constraint region.">Solution = first contact of contour and region</Step>

<Step drive="click:chip1; s1=0.2/1.5; s1=3/3" say="The disc is round, so contact is almost never exactly on an axis.">L2 disc: no corners, no zeros</Step>

<Step drive="click:chip2; s1=0.2/1.5; s1=2.2/3" say="The diamond has corners on the axes, and the ellipse often hits a corner first. Drag the centre near an axis and try both.">L1 diamond: corners on the axes → exact zeros</Step>

<div slot="visual"><L1L2Geometry client:visible lang="en" /></div>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="Least squares is a projection" say="The normal equations say the residual is orthogonal to every column.">

<Step drive="s1=1.6/2" say="With three students, y is a vector in three dimensions. The columns span a plane of possible predictions.">Columns of $X$ span the plane of possible predictions</Step>

<Step drive="s3=0.2/2.5" say="The fitted vector is the point on the plane closest to y; the residual is perpendicular.">$\hat y = Hy$, residual $\perp$ plane</Step>

<Step drive="s2=1.2/2" say="H is the hat matrix: symmetric, idempotent, with trace p.">$H = X(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top$, $H^2 = H$, $\mathrm{tr}\,H = p$</Step>

<div slot="visual"><ProjectionViz client:visible lang="en" /></div>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Variance, and how ridge buys it back" say="Now the statistics.">

<Step say="Under Gaussian noise, least squares is the maximum-likelihood estimator, with covariance sigma squared times X transpose X inverse.">$\mathrm{Cov}(\hat\beta) = \sigma^2(X^\top X)^{-1}$</Step>

<Step say="Collinear columns give X transpose X a tiny eigenvalue, so the variance explodes along that direction.">Collinear ⇒ tiny eigenvalue ⇒ huge variance</Step>

<Step say="In the SVD basis, ridge shrinks each direction by d squared over d squared plus lambda. Small singular values shrink most.">Ridge: shrink $u_j$ by $\dfrac{d_j^2}{d_j^2+\lambda}$</Step>

<Step say="Penalties are priors: Gaussian gives ridge, Laplace gives the lasso.">Gaussian prior → ridge · Laplace prior → lasso</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Soft thresholding" say="How do we actually solve the lasso?">

<Step say="Optimality: every active feature has correlation with the residual exactly lambda; every zero feature at most lambda.">KKT: $|x_j^\top r|/n \le \lambda$, $=$ if $\beta_j \ne 0$</Step>

<Step say="One coordinate at a time, the solution is soft thresholding: subtract lambda, and clip to zero.">

$$
S(z, \lambda) = \mathrm{sign}(z)\max(|z| - \lambda, 0)
$$

</Step>

<Step say="Cycle through the coordinates, warm-start from large lambda to small, and you have the whole path.">Coordinate descent + warm start = the whole path</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="cases" layout="center" title="Worked example: orthonormal design" say="With orthogonal standardized features, each estimator acts coordinate by coordinate. Take z equals three, zero point eight, minus zero point three, and lambda one half.">

<Step say="Ridge divides by one point five: two, zero point five three, minus zero point two.">Ridge $z/(1+\lambda)$: $(2,\ 0.53,\ -0.2)$</Step>

<Step say="The lasso subtracts one half and clips: two point five, zero point three, zero.">Lasso $S(z, \lambda)$: $(2.5,\ 0.3,\ 0)$</Step>

<Step say="Best subset keeps only values above one: three, zero, zero.">Best subset (hard threshold): $(3,\ 0,\ 0)$</Step>

</Slide>
