<Slide lang="en" part="method" layout="center" title="Two ways to ensemble" say="There are two routes to an ensemble.">

<Step say="Bagging works in parallel with deep, independent trees, and mainly reduces variance.">**Bagging / random forests**: parallel · deep trees · cut **variance**</Step>

<Step say="Boosting works in sequence with shallow trees, each correcting the previous one, and mainly reduces bias.">**Boosting**: sequential · shallow trees · cut **bias**</Step>

<Step say="In the next part we show that AdaBoost, which looks like a heuristic, is actually minimizing an exponential loss.">Next: AdaBoost is really minimizing an **exponential loss**</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="Tool: entropy and the Gini index" say="First, define what 'pure' means.">

<Step say="Entropy is zero for a single class and largest when the classes are uniform.">Entropy $H(D) = -\sum_k p_k\log_2 p_k$</Step>

<Step say="Information gain is how much a split lowers the entropy.">Information gain $g = H(D) - \sum_v \frac{|D_v|}{|D|}H(D_v)$</Step>

<Step say="But it favours features with many values, such as a patient ID, so C4.5 uses the gain ratio instead.">Biased toward many-valued features (e.g. patient ID) ⇒ gain ratio</Step>

<Step say="The Gini index for two classes is 2p times 1 minus p, a cheap stand-in for entropy and the CART default.">Gini $1-\sum p_k^2 = 2p(1-p)$ for two classes</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="Why averaging reduces variance" say="Let each tree have variance sigma squared and pairwise correlation rho.">

<Step effect="draw" say="For an average of B trees, the second term vanishes as B grows, but the first term, rho sigma squared, does not.">

$$
\mathrm{Var}\Big(\frac1B\sum_b f_b\Big) = \rho\sigma^2 + \frac{1-\rho}{B}\sigma^2
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="focus" say="So the key is to lower the correlation between trees, which is exactly why random forests pick features at random.">
<p class="big-q sm">Lower the correlation $\rho$ ⇒ random features</p>
</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="What AdaBoost really is" say="Write the ensemble as an additive model, add one term per round, and optimize under the exponential loss.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="All earlier rounds are folded into the sample weights.">

$$
\sum_i w_{mi}\, e^{-\alpha y_i G(x_i)}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Split the sum into correct and wrong predictions.">

$$
e^{-\alpha}\sum_{y_i = G(x_i)} w_{mi} + e^{\alpha}\sum_{y_i \ne G(x_i)} w_{mi}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="The two groups weigh one minus e and e: a simple function of the weighted error e.">

$$
e^{-\alpha}(1-e_m) + e^{\alpha}e_m
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="In G: pick the classifier with the smallest weighted error. In alpha: the derivative gives half the log-odds.">⇒ $G_m$ minimizes the weighted error, $\alpha_m = \tfrac12\ln\frac{1-e_m}{e_m}$</Step>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="Gradient boosting: fit the negative gradient" say="Now generalize the idea to any differentiable loss.">

<Step say="Each round computes the negative gradient, fits a regression tree to it, and adds it to the model scaled by the learning rate.">$r_{mi} = -\partial L/\partial F$, $F_m = F_{m-1} + \nu h_m$</Step>

<Step say="For squared loss the negative gradient is the residual, so each round fits the residuals.">Squared loss: $r = y - F$, the **residual**</Step>

<Step say="XGBoost and LightGBM add second derivatives, which gives the optimal leaf weight in closed form.">Second-order expansion ⇒ $w^* = -G/(H+\lambda)$</Step>

<Step drive="s1=1/1; s1=100%/6" say="On the right, watch gradient boosting eat away the residuals round by round.">Right: the residuals get eaten away round by round →</Step>

<Slide lang="en" part="cases" title="Worked example: eight points, three stumps" say="Let's run AdaBoost by hand on eight points labelled plus plus minus minus plus plus minus minus.">

<Step say="Round one: cut at 2.5, two mistakes, error one quarter, alpha equals half of ln 3.">① $t=2.5$: $e_1=\tfrac14$, $\alpha_1=\tfrac12\ln3$</Step>

<Step say="After the update, the misclassified points carry exactly half the weight, so the stump just used is a coin flip under the new weights.">The misclassified points' total weight → $\tfrac12$</Step>

<Step say="Round two: cut at 6.5, error one sixth, alpha equals half of ln 5.">② $t=6.5$: $e_2=\tfrac16$, $\alpha_2=\tfrac12\ln5$</Step>

<Step say="Round three: a reversed cut at 4.5, error one fifth, alpha equals ln 2.">③ $t=4.5$ (reversed): $e_3=\tfrac15$, $\alpha_3=\ln2$</Step>

<Step effect="focus" say="Three stumps that can each cut only once combine into a classifier that needs three cuts, with zero training error.">
<p class="big-q sm">Three stumps ⇒ a three-cut classifier with zero training error</p>
</Step>
