<Slide lang="en" part="method" layout="center" title="The rule of reverse mode" say="Write the process down as one rule.">

<Step say="Forward: compute and store the values in topological order.">① Forward: compute and keep every value in topological order</Step>

<Step say="Backward, in reverse order: for each node, sum the downstream gradients times the local derivatives.">② Backward: $\bar v = \sum_{c} \bar c\, \partial c / \partial v$</Step>

<Step effect="focus" say="Each node is computed once and shared with all its inputs: the chain rule plus dynamic programming, at the cost of about one forward pass.">
<p class="big-q sm">Backprop = chain rule + dynamic programming</p>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="The recursion for an MLP" say="Now for a multilayer perceptron.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="Define the error signal delta l, the derivative of the loss with respect to layer l's pre-activation.">

$$
\delta_l = \frac{\partial L}{\partial z_l}, \qquad z_{l+1} = W_{l+1}\, \varphi(z_l) + b_{l+1}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="z l reaches the loss only through the next layer; the chain rule gives the recursion.">

$$
\delta_l = \varphi'(z_l) \odot \big(W_{l+1}^\top \delta_{l+1}\big)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="A weight's gradient is the error signal times the previous layer's activation.">

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W_l} = \delta_l\, h_{l-1}^\top, \qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial b_l} = \delta_l
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Why gradients vanish" say="Unroll the recursion.">

<Step say="The first layer's error signal is a long product of matrices.">$\delta_1 = \mathrm{diag}(\varphi'_1) W_2^\top \cdots \mathrm{diag}(\varphi'_{L-1}) W_L^\top \delta_L$</Step>

<Step say="If each layer multiplies by rho on average, the first layer gets rho to the L.">Each layer $\times \rho$ ⇒ overall $\times \rho^L$</Step>

<Step say="The sigmoid's slope is at most a quarter: ten layers make it ten to the minus six.">Sigmoid: $(\frac14)^{10} \approx 9.5 \times 10^{-7}$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Keeping the variance: He init" say="How does the variance travel through a ReLU network?">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="A sum of n independent terms: the variances add.">

$$
\mathrm{Var}(z_{l+1}) = n\, \sigma_w^2\, \mathbb E\big[h_l^2\big]
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="ReLU cuts off half: the expected square of h is half the variance.">

$$
\mathrm{Var}(z_{l+1}) = \frac{n\, \sigma_w^2}{2}\, \mathrm{Var}(z_l)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Make the factor one.">

$$
\sigma_w^2 = \frac{2}{n}
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="That is He initialisation. tanh has no factor one half; Xavier uses two over the sum of the input and output widths.">He: $2/n$　Xavier: $2/(n_{\text{in}} + n_{\text{out}})$</Step>

</Slide>
