<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Gaussian ⇒ squared loss" say="Write out the Gaussian's negative log-likelihood.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="The Gaussian density.">

$$
\Pr(y \mid \mu, \sigma) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma} e^{-(y - \mu)^2 / 2\sigma^2}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Take minus the log.">

$$
-\log \Pr = \frac{(y - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} + \log \sigma + \tfrac12 \log 2\pi
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="With sigma fixed only the squared term matters: least squares is maximum likelihood under Gaussian noise.">

$$
-\log \Pr \;\propto\; (y - \mu)^2 + \text{const}
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Bernoulli ⇒ binary cross-entropy" say="Now the Bernoulli.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="p is sigmoid of f.">

$$
\ell = -y \log \sigma(f) - (1 - y) \log\big(1 - \sigma(f)\big)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Simplify, into a numerically stable form.">

$$
\ell = \log(1 + e^{f}) - y f
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Differentiate: predicted probability minus observation.">

$$
\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial f} = \sigma(f) - y
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="One gradient for all" say="Put the gradients of the different heads side by side.">

<Step say="Gaussian: mu minus y.">Gaussian: $\mu - y$</Step>

<Step say="Bernoulli: sigma of f minus y. Softmax: p minus the one-hot. Poisson: e to the f minus y.">Bernoulli $\sigma(f) - y$ · softmax $p - e_y$ · Poisson $e^f - y$</Step>

<Step effect="focus" say="The gradient is always the predicted mean minus the observation: the shared trait of the exponential family with its canonical links.">
<p class="big-q sm">Gradient = predicted mean − observation</p>
</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="The best variance" say="What variance does the network learn?">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="Let s be log sigma and write the loss.">

$$
\ell = \frac{(y - \mu)^2}{2 e^{2s}} + s
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Differentiate in s.">

$$
\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial s} = 1 - \frac{(y - \mu)^2}{e^{2s}}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Set it to zero: the variance equals the squared residual.">

$$
\sigma^2 = (y - \mu)^2
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Cross-entropy = entropy + KL" say="The average negative log-likelihood tends to the cross-entropy.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="The definition of cross-entropy.">

$$
H(p, q) = -\sum_y p(y) \log q(y)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Add and subtract a term: the entropy, plus the KL divergence.">

$$
H(p, q) = H(p) + \mathrm{KL}(p \,\|\, q)
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="By Jensen's inequality the KL is at least zero, and zero only when q equals p.">$\mathrm{KL}(p\|q) \ge 0$, with equality iff $q = p$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="The part that won't go away" say="Drag the model's prediction q and watch the cross-entropy split in two.">

<Step drive="s2=0.15/2" say="q far from p: a large KL.">$q$ far from $p$: large KL</Step>

<Step drive="s2=0.7/2" say="q equals p: the KL is zero and the cross-entropy drops to the entropy, a floor no model can go below.">$q = p$: cross-entropy = entropy</Step>

<Step drive="s1=0.95/2; s2=0.95/1.5" say="The more certain the data, the smaller the entropy and the lower the floor.">$p$ near $0$ or $1$: small entropy</Step>
