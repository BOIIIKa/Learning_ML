<Slide lang="en" part="method" layout="center" title="Generative classifiers" say="Model, strategy, algorithm, once more.">

<Step say="Model: the class prior times the class-conditional density, which is the joint distribution.">**Model**: $p(x, y) = \pi_y\, p(x \mid y)$</Step>

<Step say="Strategy: maximize the joint likelihood, and predict the class with the largest posterior.">**Strategy**: max joint likelihood; $\hat y = \arg\max_k \pi_k\, p(x\mid k)$</Step>

<Step say="Algorithm: counting and averaging; every parameter has a closed form.">**Algorithm**: counting and averaging, closed form</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Shared covariance ⇒ a line" say="Look at the log-odds of the two classes.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="The log-odds are the log prior ratio plus the log density ratio.">

$$
\log\frac{P(1\mid x)}{P(0\mid x)} = \log\frac{\pi_1}{\pi_0} + \log\frac{p(x\mid 1)}{p(x\mid 0)}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Plug in Gaussians with a shared covariance; the determinant terms cancel.">

$$
\log\frac{P(1\mid x)}{P(0\mid x)} = \log\frac{\pi_1}{\pi_0} - \tfrac12(x-\mu_1)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu_1) + \tfrac12(x-\mu_0)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu_0)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Expand: the quadratic terms in x cancel too, leaving a linear term and a constant.">

$$
\log\frac{P(1\mid x)}{P(0\mid x)} = (\mu_1-\mu_0)^\top\Sigma^{-1}x + b
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Call w the inverse covariance times the difference of the means.">

$$
\log\frac{P(1\mid x)}{P(0\mid x)} = w^\top x + b, \quad w = \Sigma^{-1}(\mu_1-\mu_0)
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step effect="focus" say="So the LDA posterior is exactly the sigmoid of w x plus b: the same model family as logistic regression.">
<p class="big-q sm">LDA posterior = $\sigma(w^\top x + b)$</p>
</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Same family, different estimators" say="So what separates LDA from logistic regression?">

<Step say="LDA maximizes the joint likelihood and computes w and b directly from means and covariances.">LDA: maximize $\sum_i \log p(x_i, y_i)$, closed form</Step>

<Step say="Logistic regression maximizes the conditional likelihood: it only cares about the boundary and assumes nothing about x.">Logistic: maximize $\sum_i \log P(y_i\mid x_i)$, iterative</Step>

<Step say="A 1-D example: means 0 and 2, variance 1, follow-up prior 0.2. The threshold moves from the midpoint 1 to 1 plus ln 2, about 1.69.">$\mu_0 = 0,\ \mu_1 = 2,\ \sigma^2 = 1,\ \pi_1 = 0.2$: $x^* = 1 + \ln 2 \approx 1.69$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="method" title="QDA: each class its own shape" say="What if the two covariances differ?">

<Step drive="s2=1.6/3; s1=90/3" say="The quadratic term no longer cancels, and the boundary becomes an ellipse, hyperbola or parabola.">$-\tfrac12 x^\top(\Sigma_1^{-1}-\Sigma_0^{-1})x$ survives → a conic</Step>

<Step say="Look at the test errors below: when the shapes really differ, QDA beats LDA.">Different shapes: QDA has the lower test error</Step>

<Step drive="click:chip1" say="But QDA estimates a whole extra covariance matrix per class. With few samples LDA is more stable: bias versus variance again.">More parameters → more variance: bias–variance</Step>

<Slide lang="en" part="method" title="Naive Bayes: just count" say="With thousands of features, even one covariance matrix is too much to estimate.">

<Step say="Naive Bayes assumes the features are independent given the class. Parameters drop from p squared to p.">$p(x\mid k) = \prod_j p(x_j\mid k)$: $O(p)$ parameters</Step>

<Step drive="click:chip1; s1=0/2" say="A new patient with neither fever nor cough. Unsmoothed, the follow-up group never showed no cough, and one zero multiplies the posterior to zero.">A zero count ⇒ posterior $= 0$</Step>

<Step drive="s1=1/2" say="Laplace smoothing pretends every cell was seen once more. The posterior becomes 0.073.">$\alpha = 1$: $P(+\mid \neg F, \neg C) = 32/437 \approx 0.073$</Step>

<Step drive="click:chip2" say="For fever and cough it moves from 0.9 to 0.798: smoothing also tempers conclusions drawn from a small sample.">$P(+\mid F, C)$: $0.9 \to 320/401 \approx 0.798$</Step>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Laplace smoothing is a MAP estimate" say="Where does smoothing come from? Put a Beta prior on the probability theta.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="m of the n samples are one; this is the likelihood.">

$$
\theta^{m}(1-\theta)^{n-m}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Multiply by a Beta prior, as if we had already seen alpha ones and alpha zeros.">

$$
\theta^{m}(1-\theta)^{n-m}\cdot\theta^{\alpha}(1-\theta)^{\alpha}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Combine the exponents.">

$$
\theta^{m+\alpha}(1-\theta)^{n-m+\alpha}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Take the mode: exactly Laplace smoothing.">

$$
\hat\theta = \frac{m+\alpha}{n+2\alpha}
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="As in Chapter 3: a penalty is a prior, and so is smoothing.">Smoothing = a prior (the Chapter 03 story again)</Step>

</Slide>
