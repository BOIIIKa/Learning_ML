<Slide lang="en" part="method" layout="center" title="One hidden unit = one hinge" say="Start with a single hidden unit.">

<Step say="An affine map, then cut off the negative part. That is a ReLU unit.">$h = \max(0,\ \theta_0 + \theta_1 x)$</Step>

<Step say="It bends where theta zero plus theta one x equals zero: off on one side, on on the other.">Kink at $x = -\theta_0/\theta_1$: "off" on one side, "on" on the other</Step>

<Step say="The network's output is a weighted sum of these units.">$y = \phi_0 + \sum_d \phi_d h_d$</Step>

<Step effect="focus" say="Once the on/off pattern is fixed, the network is linear. So a ReLU network is piecewise linear.">
<p class="big-q sm">Fixed on/off pattern ⇒ linear: ReLU nets are piecewise linear</p>
</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="method" title="Two inputs: lines carve polygons" say="With two inputs, each unit draws a line on the plane.">

<Step drive="s1=1/0.6" say="One unit, one line, two regions; on each the network is a single linear function.">$D = 1$: two regions</Step>

<Step drive="s1=3/2" say="Three units, three lines, at most seven regions.">$D = 3$: at most $7$</Step>

<Step drive="s1=5/2" say="Five units, sixteen regions. The count grows quadratically with the units.">$D = 5$: $16$</Step>

<Step drive="click:chip1; wait:1.5" say="With new weights some crossings fall outside the window and fewer regions show. The bound is one plus D plus D choose two.">Bound $1 + D + \binom{D}{2}$</Step>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="One input: at most D + 1 pieces" say="Why do D units give at most D plus one pieces?">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="The output is a weighted sum of the units.">

$$
f(x) = \phi_0 + \sum_{d=1}^{D} \phi_d \max(0,\ \theta_{d0} + \theta_{d1} x)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Each unit changes the slope only at its own kink.">

$$
f'(x) \text{ changes only at } x_d = -\theta_{d0}/\theta_{d1}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="D points cut the real line into at most D plus one pieces.">

$$
\#\text{pieces} \le D + 1
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="Conversely, any broken line can be written this way: the output weights are the changes of slope at the kinks.">Conversely: $\phi_d$ = change of slope at kink $d$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Two inputs: counting regions" say="How do we count regions in two dimensions? Add the lines one at a time.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="Line k crosses the previous k minus one lines at k minus one points, is cut into k segments, and each segment adds a region.">

$$
\#\text{regions} = 1 + \sum_{k=1}^{D} k
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Sum it up.">

$$
\#\text{regions} = 1 + D + \frac{D(D-1)}{2}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="That is one plus D plus D choose two.">

$$
\#\text{regions} = 1 + D + \binom{D}{2}
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="With D i inputs it is the sum of D choose j for j up to D i: higher dimension, more regions.">$D_i$ inputs: $\sum_{j=0}^{D_i} \binom{D}{j}$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="The universal approximation theorem" say="A single hidden layer can approximate any continuous function. How?">

<Step say="Interpolate with a broken line at equally spaced knots: that broken line is itself a one-hidden-layer ReLU network.">Broken-line interpolant = one-hidden-layer ReLU net</Step>

<Step say="For an L-Lipschitz function with knot spacing h, the error is at most L times h over two.">Error $\le Lh/2$, $h = (b - a)/D$</Step>

<Step effect="focus" say="But it does not say how many units, exponentially many in high dimension; nor how to find the parameters; nor whether it generalizes.">
<p class="big-q sm">Silent on: how many units · how to train · generalization</p>
</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="method" title="Depth as folding" say="The tent map uses two ReLUs to fold the interval in half.">

<Step drive="s1=1/0.6" say="One layer: one fold, two pieces.">$L = 1$: $2$ pieces</Step>

<Step drive="s1=2/1.5" say="Two layers: two folds, four pieces.">$L = 2$: $4$ pieces</Step>

<Step drive="s1=3/1.5" say="Three layers: eight pieces. A shallow net with the same parameters has at most seven: it already falls behind.">$L = 3$: $8 \gt 7$</Step>

<Step drive="s1=6/3" say="Six layers: sixty-four pieces, against at most thirteen for the shallow net of the same size.">$L = 6$: $64$ against $13$</Step>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Why folding gives 2^L pieces" say="Write the tent map out.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="Two ReLU units.">

$$
t(x) = 2\max(0, x) - 4\max\!\left(0, x - \tfrac12\right)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="It maps each half one-to-one onto the whole interval.">

$$
t:\ [0, \tfrac12] \to [0, 1],\quad [\tfrac12, 1] \to [0, 1]
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="So every composition doubles the number of pieces.">

$$
\#\text{pieces}\big(t^{(L)}\big) = 2 \cdot \#\text{pieces}\big(t^{(L-1)}\big) = 2^L
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="The deep net has six L plus one parameters; a shallow net of the same size has only two L units, so at most two L plus one pieces.">Deep: $6L + 1$ parameters → $2^L$ pieces; shallow: at most $2L + 1$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="cases" layout="center" title="Worked example: reading off a broken line" say="A three-unit network by hand.">

<Step say="Three hinges, with kinks at zero, one and two.">$f = 1 + 2\,\mathrm{ReLU}(x) - 3\,\mathrm{ReLU}(x - 1) + 2\,\mathrm{ReLU}(x - 2)$</Step>

<Step say="Accumulate the slopes from left to right: zero, two, minus one, one.">Slopes: $0 \to 2 \to -1 \to 1$</Step>

<Step say="The values at zero, one, two and three are one, three, two, three. Four pieces: exactly D plus one.">$f(0, 1, 2, 3) = 1, 3, 2, 3$: $4 = D + 1$ pieces</Step>
