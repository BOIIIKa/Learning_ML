<Slide lang="en" part="method" title="Expected and empirical risk" say="First, make 'good' precise.">

<Step say="A loss function measures the cost of one prediction.">Loss $L(y, f(x))$: the cost of one prediction</Step>

<Step say="The expected risk is the average loss over the whole distribution. It is the real target, but the distribution is unknown, so we cannot compute it.">Expected risk $R(f) = \mathbb E[L(Y, f(X))]$: the real target, but not computable</Step>

<Step say="The empirical risk is the average loss on the data in hand, a stand-in we can compute.">Empirical risk $\hat R_n(f) = \frac1n\sum_i L(y_i, f(x_i))$: a computable stand-in</Step>

<Step say="Minimizing the empirical risk is called ERM. Adding a complexity penalty gives structural risk minimization, SRM.">ERM: $\min \hat R_n$ · SRM: $\min \hat R_n + \lambda J(f)$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="The best prediction under squared loss" say="If we knew the true distribution, what would the best prediction be?">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="Fix x and predict Y with a constant c. Look at the expected squared loss.">

$$
\mathbb E[(Y-c)^2]
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Subtract and add the mean μ inside the bracket.">

$$
\mathbb E[(Y-\mu+\mu-c)^2]
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Expand the square. In the cross term, Y minus μ has expectation zero.">

$$
\mathbb E[(Y-\mu)^2] + 2(\mu-c)\,\mathbb E[Y-\mu] + (\mu-c)^2
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Two terms remain: the variance, plus the squared gap between the mean and c.">

$$
\mathrm{Var}(Y) + (\mu-c)^2
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="The first term does not depend on c; it is irreducible noise. The second term vanishes when c equals the conditional mean.">⇒ $f^*(x) = \mathbb E[Y\mid X = x]$</Step>

<Step say="Likewise, the best prediction under absolute loss is the conditional median. That is why the two lines differ: the absentees drag the mean down but barely move the median.">Absolute loss ⇒ the conditional **median**</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="The Bayes classifier" say="Classification uses the 0-1 loss.">

<Step say="At each x, predicting k has expected loss one minus its posterior, so we should pick the class with the largest posterior.">$f^*(x) = \arg\max_k\ p(x\mid k)\,P(k)$</Step>

<Step say="Its error rate is the Bayes error, a floor no classifier can break.">Bayes error: the floor for every classifier</Step>

<Step drive="s1=-2/2.5; s1=2.2/4; s1=0.4/2" say="Drag your threshold on the right: wherever you put it, the error rate never drops below its value at the Bayes threshold.">Drag the threshold; you never beat Bayes →</Step>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="Can empirical risk stand in for expected risk?" say="For a fixed function, the law of large numbers makes the empirical risk converge to the expected risk.">

<Step say="On the right, we predict an exponential variable with a constant under absolute loss. Purple is the expected risk, orange the empirical risk.">Purple = expected risk, orange = empirical risk →</Step>

<Step drive="s1=0.6/1.5; s1=3.2/4.5" say="With few samples the two minimizers are far apart; with many, the ERM solution approaches the optimum.">As $n$ grows, the ERM solution → the optimum</Step>
