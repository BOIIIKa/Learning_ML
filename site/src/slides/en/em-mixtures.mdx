<Slide lang="en" part="method" layout="center" title="Gaussian mixture models" say="The model, the E-step, the M-step.">

<Step say="First pick a component by its weight, then generate the data from that Gaussian.">$p(x) = \sum_k \pi_k\,\mathcal N(x; \mu_k, \Sigma_k)$</Step>

<Step say="E-step: Bayes' rule gives the responsibilities.">E-step: $r_{ik} \propto \pi_k\,\mathcal N(x_i; \mu_k, \Sigma_k)$</Step>

<Step say="M-step: use the responsibilities as weights and estimate as if labels were known.">M-step: $\pi_k = N_k/n$, $\mu_k = \sum_i r_{ik}x_i / N_k$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="The ELBO decomposition" say="For any distribution q, the log-likelihood splits into two parts.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="q sums to one, so the log-likelihood can be written as an expectation under q.">

$$
\log p(x) = \sum_z q(z)\log p(x)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="p of x is the joint divided by the posterior.">

$$
\log p(x) = \sum_z q(z)\log\frac{p(x, z)}{p(z\mid x)}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Multiply and divide by the same q and split into two terms.">

$$
\log p(x) = \sum_z q(z)\log\frac{p(x, z)}{q(z)} + \sum_z q(z)\log\frac{q(z)}{p(z\mid x)}
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="The first term is the ELBO; the second is a KL divergence, never negative.">

$$
\log p(x) = \mathrm{ELBO}(q, \theta) + \mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,p(z\mid x))
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step effect="focus" say="Since the KL is non-negative, the ELBO is a lower bound on the log-likelihood, tight when q is the posterior.">
<p class="big-q sm">ELBO ≤ log p, equal when q is the posterior</p>
</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="Touch, then push" say="EM is coordinate ascent on the ELBO.">

<Step drive="s1=0/0.6" say="The E-step sets q to the posterior at the current parameter, and the blue lower bound touches the yellow log-likelihood at theta t.">E-step: KL = 0, the bound touches at $\theta_t$</Step>

<Step drive="s1=1/1.5" say="The M-step jumps to the bound's highest point, and the log-likelihood is pushed up with it.">M-step: maximize the ELBO, $\log p$ rises too</Step>

<Step drive="s1=8/4" say="Touch and push, again and again, until it stops at a local maximum. Raising the bound is enough; that is generalized EM.">$\log p(\theta_{t+1}) \ge \mathrm{ELBO}(\theta_{t+1}) \ge \mathrm{ELBO}(\theta_t) = \log p(\theta_t)$</Step>
