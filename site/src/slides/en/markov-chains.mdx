<Slide lang="en" part="method" layout="center" title="Markov chains and stationary distributions" say="First, a reminder about Markov chains.">

<Step say="The next step depends only on the current state, described by a transition matrix P.">$P(X_{t+1} = j \mid X_t = i) = P_{ij}$</Step>

<Step say="A distribution pi that P leaves unchanged is stationary.">Stationary: $\pi^\top P = \pi^\top$</Step>

<Step say="If the chain is irreducible and aperiodic, pi is unique, the chain converges to it from anywhere, and time averages converge to expectations.">Irreducible + aperiodic ⇒ unique, convergent, $\frac1T\sum f(X_t) \to \mathbb E_\pi f$</Step>

<Step effect="focus" say="MCMC runs this backwards: fix pi first, then build a chain with pi as its stationary distribution.">
<p class="big-q sm">Fix π first, then build the chain</p>
</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Detailed balance ⇒ stationarity" say="How do we guarantee that pi is stationary? A sufficient condition.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="Detailed balance: between any two states, the probability flow is the same both ways.">

$$
\pi(x)\,T(x \to x') = \pi(x')\,T(x' \to x)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Sum both sides over x.">

$$
\sum_x \pi(x)\,T(x \to x') = \pi(x')\sum_x T(x' \to x)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="The transition probabilities out of x prime sum to one, so pi is stationary.">

$$
\sum_x \pi(x)\,T(x \to x') = \pi(x')
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="method" layout="center" title="Metropolis–Hastings" say="The M–H algorithm satisfies detailed balance by design.">

<Step say="Draw a candidate from the proposal q.">Propose $x'$ from $q(x' \mid x)$</Step>

<Step say="Accept with this probability. The normalizing constant cancels in the ratio, so only the unnormalized density is needed.">$\alpha = \min\!\left(1, \frac{\tilde\pi(x')q(x \mid x')}{\tilde\pi(x)q(x' \mid x)}\right)$</Step>

<Step say="On rejection, stay put, and record the current point again.">Reject ⇒ record $x$ again</Step>

<Step say="Check: pi times q times alpha is the smaller of the two terms, which is symmetric in x and x prime, so detailed balance holds.">$\pi(x)q(x' \mid x)\alpha = \min\big(\pi(x)q(x' \mid x),\ \pi(x')q(x \mid x')\big)$ is symmetric</Step>

<Step say="Gibbs sampling is a special case: draw from the conditional, and the acceptance is always one.">Gibbs: draw from $\pi(x_j \mid x_{-j})$, $\alpha \equiv 1$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Why PageRank is unique" say="Write it as a matrix equation.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="PageRank satisfies this linear equation.">

$$
r = \frac{1 - d}{n}\mathbf 1 + d\,M r
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Since r sums to one, this is a matrix G times r.">

$$
r = \Big(\frac{1 - d}{n}\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top + dM\Big) r = G r
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="Every entry of G is strictly positive and its columns sum to one. By Perron–Frobenius, the eigenvalue one has a unique positive eigenvector.">$G \gt 0$, column-stochastic ⇒ Perron–Frobenius: unique</Step>

<Step say="The other eigenvalues have modulus at most d, which sets the speed of power iteration.">Power-iteration error $\sim |\lambda_2|^t$, $|\lambda_2| \le d$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="cases" layout="center" title="Worked example: PageRank of three pages" say="A small web by hand.">

<Step say="A links to B and C, B links to C, C links to A, and d is zero point eight five.">$A \to B, C$; $B \to C$; $C \to A$; $d = 0.85$</Step>

<Step say="The equations: every page gets zero point zero five, plus a share from each page linking to it.">$r_A = 0.05 + 0.85 r_C$, $r_B = 0.05 + 0.425 r_A$, $r_C = 0.05 + 0.425 r_A + 0.85 r_B$</Step>

<Step say="Solving, C ranks highest, because both A and B vote for it.">$r = (686, 380, 703)/1769 \approx (0.388, 0.215, 0.397)$</Step>

<Step say="Power iteration oscillates into the answer: the other two eigenvalues are a complex pair of modulus zero point six.">Iterates: $(0.333, 0.192, 0.475) \to (0.454, 0.192, 0.355) \to \cdots$, $|\lambda_2| \approx 0.601$</Step>
