<Slide lang="en" part="method" layout="center" title="k nearest neighbours" say="The three ingredients of kNN.">

<Step say="Model: find the k closest points; classify by majority vote, regress by averaging.">**Model**: $\hat y(x) = \arg\max_c \sum_{i\in N_k(x)} \mathbb 1[y_i = c]$</Step>

<Step say="Three choices: the distance, k, and the decision rule.">**Three choices**: distance $\|x - z\|_p$ · $k$ · voting rule</Step>

<Step say="There is no training; all the work happens at prediction time. And always standardize the features first.">No training; **standardize first**</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="method" title="kd-trees: split, then backtrack" say="Plain kNN computes n distances per prediction. A kd-tree cuts the plane recursively.">

<Step drive="s1=0/0.6; s1=3/3" say="The root splits at the median x, the next level at the median y, then x again; each node owns a rectangle.">Cycle the coordinates, split at the median</Step>

<Step drive="s2=0/0.6; s2=100%/5" say="Search first walks down to the query's leaf. On the way back it searches the other side only if the circle crosses the splitting line; otherwise the whole subtree is pruned.">Backtrack: cross the line or prune</Step>

<Step drive="click:chip2; s2=100%/4" say="Another query visits different nodes. In low dimension a search visits about log n nodes on average.">About $O(\log n)$ nodes per search</Step>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Cover–Hart: how good is the nearest neighbour?" say="With infinitely many samples, the 1-NN error is controlled by the Bayes error.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="The labels of x and its nearest neighbour x prime are independent, and 1-NN errs exactly when they differ.">

$$
P(Y \ne Y' \mid x) = \eta(1 - \eta') + (1 - \eta)\eta'
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="With infinitely many samples, x prime tends to x and eta prime to eta.">

$$
P(Y \ne Y' \mid x) \to 2\eta(1 - \eta)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Rewrite it with the Bayes error rate r star.">

$$
2\eta(1 - \eta) = 2r^*(1 - r^*)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Take expectations and apply Jensen's inequality: the Cover–Hart bound.">

$$
R_{1\text{NN}} \le 2R^*(1 - R^*) \le 2R^*
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step effect="focus" say="The simplest rule is never worse than twice the optimal classifier. But only with infinite data.">
<p class="big-q sm">Nearest neighbour ≤ twice the Bayes error (asymptotically)</p>
</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="The curse of dimensionality" say="In high dimension, though, the nearest neighbour is not near.">

<Step drive="s1=1/0.6" say="In two dimensions the nearest point is much closer than the farthest.">$d = 2$: nearest ≪ farthest</Step>

<Step drive="s1=11/5" say="At two hundred dimensions the ratio approaches one: everyone is equally far, and local loses its meaning.">$d$ in the hundreds: nearest / farthest $\to 1$</Step>

<Step say="Almost all the volume sits in a thin outer shell, and a cube holding ten percent of the data needs an edge nearly as long as the whole cube.">Shell fraction $1 - (1-\varepsilon)^d$; edge $r^{1/d}$</Step>

<Slide lang="en" part="method" title="Splines: piecewise polynomials" say="For regression, expand x into basis functions, then run a linear regression.">

<Step drive="s1=0/0.6" say="With no knots it is a single cubic.">$K = 0$: one cubic</Step>

<Step drive="s1=12/5" say="Add knots: the spline adjusts locally, while a single polynomial with the same degrees of freedom swings wildly at the ends.">Splines adjust locally; polynomials oscillate</Step>

<Step drive="s1=5/2" say="The basis functions are the positive part of x minus xi, cubed; each one acts only to the right of its knot.">Basis: $1, x, x^2, x^3, (x - \xi_k)^3_+$</Step>

<div slot="visual"><SplineVsPoly client:visible lang="en" /></div>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Degrees of freedom of a cubic spline" say="Count the parameters.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="K knots make K plus one pieces, four parameters each.">

$$
\mathrm{df} = 4(K + 1)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Each knot requires continuous value, first and second derivative: three constraints.">

$$
\mathrm{df} = 4(K + 1) - 3K
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="That is K plus four, exactly the number of basis functions.">

$$
\mathrm{df} = K + 4
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="A smoothing spline picks no knots and controls smoothness by penalizing the integral of the squared second derivative. A GAM gives every feature its own spline and adds them up.">Smoothing spline $\lambda\int f''^2$ · GAM $\beta_0 + \sum_j f_j(x_j)$</Step>

</Slide>
