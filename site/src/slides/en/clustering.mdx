<Slide lang="en" part="method" layout="center" title="k-means" say="Model, strategy, algorithm.">

<Step say="Model: K centres and a partition.">**Model**: centres $\mu_1, \dots, \mu_K$ + a partition $c(i)$</Step>

<Step say="Strategy: the smallest within-cluster sum of squares.">**Strategy**: $J = \sum_i \|x_i - \mu_{c(i)}\|^2$</Step>

<Step say="Algorithm: assign with the centres fixed, update with the assignment fixed, in turn. That is coordinate descent.">**Algorithm**: Lloyd's alternating minimization = coordinate descent</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="Why the update takes the mean" say="Fix one cluster and see how its sum of squares depends on the centre.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="One cluster's sum of squares.">

$$
\sum_{i} \|x_i - \mu\|^2
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Insert the cluster mean, x bar.">

$$
\sum_{i} \|x_i - \bar x + \bar x - \mu\|^2
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="The cross terms sum to zero, leaving two parts.">

$$
\sum_{i} \|x_i - \bar x\|^2 + n\|\bar x - \mu\|^2
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="The second part is non-negative, so the mean is the best centre; the assignment is best for every point too. Neither step raises the objective, partitions are finite, so the algorithm must stop.">Neither step raises $J$ + finitely many partitions ⇒ stops (at a local optimum)</Step>

<Step effect="focus" say="But the objective is not convex: only a local optimum is guaranteed, and the start matters.">
<p class="big-q sm">Only a local optimum</p>
</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="math" title="How many clusters?" say="Without labels, how do we choose k?">

<Step drive="s1=1/0.6; s1=8/4" say="The within-cluster sum of squares falls with every k, down to zero at k equals n, so we look for an elbow.">Sum of squares always falls: find the "elbow"</Step>

<Step drive="s1=3/2" say="The silhouette compares the mean distance to one's own cluster with that to the nearest other cluster; it peaks at k equals three.">Silhouette $s = (b-a)/\max(a,b)$: peaks at $k = 3$</Step>
