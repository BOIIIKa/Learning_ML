<Slide lang="en" part="math" layout="center" title="PCA by Lagrange multipliers" say="Maximize the projected variance subject to unit length.">

<Morph>

<Step effect="morph" say="Objective: the projected variance. Constraint: unit length.">

$$
\max_w\ w^\top\Sigma w \quad \text{s.t.}\ w^\top w = 1
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Write the Lagrangian.">

$$
L = w^\top\Sigma w - \lambda(w^\top w - 1)
$$

</Step>

<Step effect="morph" say="Set the derivative in w to zero: the eigen-equation.">

$$
\Sigma w = \lambda w
$$

</Step>

</Morph>

<Step say="The projected variance is then lambda, so take the largest eigenvalue. Example: covariance two one, one two has eigenvalues three and one, and the first component explains seventy-five percent.">$\Sigma = [[2,1],[1,2]]$: $\lambda = 3, 1$, explains $75\%$</Step>

</Slide>

<Slide lang="en" part="method" title="Low-rank approximation" say="Treat an image as a matrix and take its singular value decomposition.">

<Step drive="s1=1/0.6" say="Each term is a rank-one image. With only the first term, all that is left is the rough shading.">$A = \sum_j \sigma_j u_j v_j^\top$</Step>

<Step drive="s1=10/4" say="The singular values fall fast; with about ten terms the picture is already clear.">The spectrum falls fast → small $k$ suffices</Step>

<Step say="The Eckart–Young theorem: the truncated SVD is the best among all matrices of rank at most k, and its error is exactly the root of the sum of the discarded squared singular values.">Eckart–Young: $\|A - A_k\|_F = \sqrt{\sum_{j>k}\sigma_j^2}$</Step>
